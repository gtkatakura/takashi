# Intake and charter

## Asking

- Read what exists first; do not ask what it already answers.
- Batches of up to four questions, each with a recommended answer and what the choice changes.
- Record each answer as given, with the date. An answer that looks unsafe or contradictory is questioned once, with the reason, then recorded as decided.

## The twelve questions

| # | Question | Without it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Which decision does this unlock, and what is not decided now? | The study answers a nearby question |
| 2 | From whose point of view is the result judged? | It is judged by our build cost, or by a simulated buyer |
| 3 | What is the unit of analysis, and how fine? | Units mirror the code and miss what a user would name |
| 4 | What is in focus, mapped for later, out? | Depth is spent on what will not be executed |
| 5 | Which floors hold whatever the study finds? | A proposal trades away something never offered |
| 6 | Which business constraints apply: licence, segment, scale, hosting? | A candidate is recommended that cannot ship |
| 7 | Which proof level is required, and may experiments run? | "It works" is stated from reading |
| 8 | Which reference sets (markets, projects, papers) matter? | The scout repeats the obvious names |
| 9 | Which budget: rounds per tranche, agents per round, models, checkpoints? | The loop stops when the session ends, not when it is done |
| 10 | Which form does the result take, and where does it live? | A page nobody asked for, or no page |
| 11 | When is it good enough to stop? | No stop criterion, so no stop |
| 12 | What is already decided and not reopened? | A closed decision is argued again |

## Charter template

```markdown
# Charter: <study>

Released by the owner on <date>. Changed only by the owner.

1. Decision — what this unlocks; what it does not decide.
2. Judged by — whose point of view, one column per kind of user.
3. Unit and axes — the unit, the map axes, the values a cell may take.
4. Scope — | In focus | Mapped for later | Out |
5. Floors — what holds whatever the study finds.
6. Premises — | Id | Premise | Class (frame, current state, market, demand, business, method) | Declared by | Evidence or HYPOTHESIS | Overturned by |
7. Proof — level per kind of cell; allowed experiments and downloads; what the machine cannot run.
8. Sources — repos at pinned commits; reference sets; demand signals.
9. Frozen material — what exists on the topic and which agents may not read it.
10. Seats — | Seat | Lens | Model | (two to four lens seats plus the fixed ones)
11. Budget and checkpoints — rounds per tranche, agents per round, ceiling.
12. Stop criteria — the standard ones plus any the study adds.
13. Deliverables — files, page, where they live.
14. Owner answers — | Date | Question | Answer, as given |
```

## Owner checkpoints

| When | Bring | Owner decides |
|---|---|---|
| Charter | The charter and the premises you are least sure of | Release or change |
| After the scout | Macro table, shortlist, what was searched and not found | What to deepen and drop |
| Divergence open two rounds | Each seat's position and evidence, the judge's recommendation | The divergence |
| End of a tranche | Result so far, each stop criterion's state, error rate, spend, continuation note | Stop or release the next tranche |
| End | Synthesis, decisions with recommendations, what is not claimed | The decisions |
